Dear Editors,

I am pleased to submit the manuscript **"What Is Behind 'People Receiving Quality Health Services'? Measurement in the World Bank Group's Health Results"** for consideration in *The World Bank Economic Review*.

The World Bank Group's FY24–FY30 Scorecard reports that 378.9 million people received quality health, nutrition and population services by June 2025. Using the public project-level records behind the Scorecard, the paper first reconciles this figure exactly to its components: 92 World Bank project results plus the IFC and MIGA aggregates. It then shows how the figure is constructed:

- only 37% of the World Bank total is a direct count of people;
- 29% comes from converting coverage percentages into people, mostly COVID-19 vaccination;
- 11% comes from converting visits, facilities or cases into people;
- 59% of the women reported reached are a fixed share of the total.

Restricting the count to people actually counted lowers the headline by a third. The paper also documents that the total is highly concentrated and that health results are much more likely to be rescaled than results in other Scorecard areas. It closes with five concrete recommendations, including reporting effective coverage and publishing a construction code for every figure.

I believe the paper fits the Review's focus on policy-relevant research on development and on the World Bank's work. It speaks directly to the measurement of universal health coverage and to the Independent Evaluation Group's ongoing formative evaluation of the Scorecard.

All data are public. Replication files in Stata and R, including a script that checks every number in the paper, will be deposited in accordance with the journal's data policy.

The manuscript has not been published and is not under consideration elsewhere. [A companion working paper, "Counting Reach, Not Welfare", uses the same data release to study all five people-level results areas; the present paper is distinct in its focus, accounting decomposition and analysis.]
